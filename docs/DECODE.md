# The decode

Nine criteria are not nine filters. They collapse into four bins, and the
sorting is the whole of step 2.

## The four bins

| Bin | What is in it | What to do with it |
|---|---|---|
| **Identity** | Taste and subculture. What they read, listen to, do on a Sunday. | These co-occur, and they have addresses. This bin becomes the rooms. |
| **Logistical** | Age, institution, schedule, distance. | These name buildings. Two of them intersecting implies a population nobody wrote down, which is why they are handled as constraints and never as a search. |
| **Inoperable** | Things you cannot observe from outside, or cannot act on without ranking people. Kindness. Ambition. "Emotionally available." | Name them, agree they are real, and set them down. They are not filters; they are things you find out on visit three. |
| **Refused** | Protected characteristics. | Not filters. Not search axes. Not a column in any table. |

## The surgery pattern

For each line of the seed: diagnosis, then what they probably mean, then a
one-line rewrite. Cite the seed line by number. Never quote it — the decode
lives in a tracked file and the seed does not.

> **Line 4.** Diagnosis: inoperable, phrased as a filter.
> What you probably mean: you have been in rooms where nobody asked you a
> question back, and you do not want that again.
> Rewrite: *rooms where the format makes people talk to each other.*

The rewrite must be actionable by a person on a Wednesday. If it is not, it
belongs in the inoperable bin, which is not a failure — a named gap is useful
and a confident guess is worse than useless.

## The tell

The justification language is the thing to watch. If a rewrite can only be
argued for by naming a category of people, it is targeting, and no amount of
padding it with a real clause changes that. `scripts/preflight.py` reads every
column of the rooms table for exactly this reason.
